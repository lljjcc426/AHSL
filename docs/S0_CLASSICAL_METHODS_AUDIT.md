# S0 classical-methods audit

## Cross-field conclusion

The main classical threat to the selected subfield is not a hypergraph algorithm. It is the combination of factorial contrasts/Möbius inversion, hierarchical sparse regression, design of experiments, and domain-specific interaction scores. Any S1 contribution that compares only with HGNNs would be scientifically incomplete.

## 1. Selected subfield: combinatorial interaction supports

### Exact contrasts and set-function expansions

With all `2^p` subset responses observed, Möbius inversion uniquely recovers every coefficient on a fixed response scale. Classical factorial ANOVA, Walsh–Hadamard/Fourier expansions on the Boolean cube, epistasis contrasts, Bliss/Loewe-type drug nulls, and inclusion–exclusion are therefore the strongest identification baselines. ML is unnecessary in the full, noiseless factorial regime.

The residual begins when observations are sparse, noisy, heterogeneous, or actively acquired. Then the number of candidate supports is exponential, and the useful assumptions become bounded order, sparsity, heredity, exchangeability, or side-information transfer.

### Sparse and hierarchical selection

The lasso, group lasso, hierarchical lasso (`hierNet`), `glinternet`, sparse log-linear models, and compressed sensing are mandatory baselines. Strong heredity can be scientifically wrong for pure higher-order effects, so both hereditary and non-hereditary estimators are required. This is a substantive scientific choice, not a tuning detail.

### Experimental design

Fractional factorial designs, D-optimal designs, group testing, sequential design, Bayesian optimization, and active learning directly address which combinations to measure. A passive predictor that ignores acquisition cost does not exhaust the subfield.

### Domain estimators

- Genetics: multiplicative fitness nulls, SGA/τ-SGA scoring, Bayesian interaction models, multifactor dimensionality reduction.
- Drug combinations: Bliss independence, Loewe additivity, Highest Single Agent, ZIP, and emergent/hidden-interaction contrasts.
- Microbial communities: generalized Lotka–Volterra baselines, bottom-up pairwise coefficients, higher-order landscape expansions.

Verdict: **classical methods are strong but do not close sparse cross-combination generalization, uncertainty, order extrapolation, or measurement selection. G5 passes.**

## 2. Temporal group-event structure

Classical baselines include common-neighbor/resource-allocation scores, simplicial closure, survival analysis, Hawkes/marked point processes, set-valued point processes, and frequent-itemset/association-rule mining. HyperSearch adds anti-monotone pruning with a score-preserving guarantee. The classical residual is smaller than papers using random-negative classification suggest.

## 3. Factor scopes and tractability constraints

Hierarchical log-linear selection, score-based graphical-model search, pseudo-likelihood, exact bounded-treewidth Bayesian-network learning, junction-tree methods, and decomposable/chordal model selection are mature. Probabilistic circuits provide tractability by construction without first recovering a factor hypergraph. This family fails the residual/saturation gate for the present program.

## 4. Dynamics-to-hypergraph reconstruction

Sparse identification of nonlinear dynamics (SINDy), polynomial libraries, regression of coupling tensors, ARNI, Granger/transfer-entropy variants, and system identification are the relevant classical families. Their main limitation is not lack of model capacity but non-identifiability under limited excitation, local linearization, latent variables, and derivative noise.

## 5. Protein complexes and latent groups

MCL, MCODE, clique percolation, ClusterONE, stochastic block models, overlapping community detection, matrix/tensor factorization, and AP-MS scoring are mature. Gold standards are incomplete and often reused in preprocessing or parameter selection. A new learner needs time/species/assay-disjoint evidence, not another random CORUM split.

## Classical kill criterion carried into S1

If an explicit Möbius/contrast estimator plus sparse hierarchical regression matches the proposed learner on gene-held-out and order-held-out real data, the ML residual is absent and the concrete S1 topic must be killed.
