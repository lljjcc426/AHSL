# R0 Publication Feasibility

## Current result

No candidate is ready as a distinct high-order learning-augmented exact-
inference paper. Zero candidates reach semifinal or finalist status.

| possible paper | plausible fit | missing evidence |
|---|---|---|
| learned VE ordering | UAI/AISTATS/TMLR only with strong cross-family result | >=3x residual over tuned classical policies on two high-order families |
| verified learned GHD separator ranking | CP/TODS/PODS/UAI | factor-bearing benchmark coupling decomposition search to exact execution |
| hypergraph-aware exact-engine selector | UAI/AIJ/TMLR | family-disjoint predictability and affordable labels beyond JoinInfer's hybrid |
| robust prediction-assisted exact inference theory | AISTATS/NeurIPS/ICALP-style theory | a concrete prediction object with nontrivial consistency/robustness tradeoff |
| benchmark/resource paper | TMLR/data/system venue | maintained solver containers, licenses, traces and repeated workload definition |

## Compute and amortization

The local gate used no GPU and a bounded dense VE subset. A larger oracle sweep
is computationally possible under the 150 CPU-hour ceiling, but is not justified
after the classical residual gate failed. Training labels would require several
orders/engines per instance and become most expensive on the high-order models
that matter.

UAI is a heterogeneous competition archive, HyperBench is a structural corpus,
and XCSP is a solver competition. None is a demonstrated recurring deployed
workload where offline strategy labeling amortizes over many future exact
queries from a stable distribution.

## Decision implication

Architecture-only novelty—adding a GNN/RL policy to min-fill, branching or join
ordering—should be rejected. Reopening requires a new external artifact that
simultaneously demonstrates classical headroom, hypergraph-feature necessity,
family-disjoint predictability and label amortization while preserving exactness.
