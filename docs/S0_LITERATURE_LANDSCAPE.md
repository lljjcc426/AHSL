## Why the selected field is genuinely high-order

The yeast study reports that more than 30% of trigenic interactions were not anticipated from doubles. The drug full-factorial study found hidden suppression in 54% of 20,790 tested dose-combinations; by definition those effects require comparison to non-single lower-order subsets. Microbial experiments show interaction coefficients reorganize as community order grows and that trio information improves seven-strain bottom-up prediction. These are direct failures of a model that stores only fixed pair effects.

## Why ML is conditional rather than assumed

For complete small factorials, exact contrasts solve the structural problem. ML becomes justified only when the candidate space is combinatorial and measurements are sparse, when entity side information supports transfer, or when experimental acquisition must be selected. S1 therefore has a classical-residual gate before any complex learner.

## Semifinals and finalists

Semifinals (5): factor-scope learning; temporal/directed group events; dynamics-to-hypergraph reconstruction; protein-complex discovery; combinatorial interaction supports.

Finalists (2):

1. sparse high-order combinatorial interaction structure learning from intervention–response data — 67/75;
2. open-world temporal/directed group-event structure forecasting — 60/75.

The first is selected because its structure labels are closer to direct experimental effects and its theory/statistics interface is clearer. The second retains broader datasets but carries greater saturation and evaluation risk.
